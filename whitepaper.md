# Whitepaper - Part 1: Game Concept and Vision

## 1. Introduction
This document outlines the game concept for a hybrid title blending Factorio-style automation with zombie wave survival, enriched by RPG elements. Players will find themselves in a hostile world where they must build intricate automated factories to produce resources, defend against increasingly dangerous zombie hordes, and manage their personal survival needs, all while exploring a world filled with lore, challenges, and opportunities for progression.

## 2. Core Game Loop

The core gameplay revolves around a continuous cycle of three key activities: Automate, Survive, and Progress.

*   **Automate:** Players begin by gathering basic raw resources such as iron ore, copper ore, stone, and wood. These materials are then processed through player-built machinery into intermediate components like iron plates, copper wires, and gears. These components are the building blocks for expanding the factory, which in turn produces essential goods for survival, facilitates research into new technologies, and manufactures defensive structures to protect against threats.

*   **Survive:** Beyond the external threats, players must manage their character's fundamental needs:
    *   **Hydration:** Securing a reliable source of water and purifying it is crucial.
    *   **Nourishment:** Players will need to hunt wildlife, cultivate crops, or scavenge for edible supplies, then cook or prepare them for consumption.
    *   **Rest:** Establishing a safe and secure location to sleep is necessary to recover stamina or energy, impacting overall effectiveness.
    *   **Shelter:** Building and maintaining a defensible base is paramount to protect against zombie attacks and potentially adverse environmental conditions.
    Periodic zombie waves will assault the player's base. The intensity, size, and types of zombies in these waves will escalate over time, influenced by game progression and other in-game events.

*   **Progress:** Advancing in the game involves several interconnected systems:
    *   **Tech Tree:** A comprehensive research system allows players to unlock new technologies. These advancements grant access to more efficient machinery, stronger construction materials, more powerful defensive options, and new tools and techniques for survival.
    *   **Exploration:** Players are encouraged to venture beyond their initial starting area to discover diverse biomes. These new zones may offer unique resources, reveal hidden locations such as pre-existing ruins or challenging dungeons, and present new environmental challenges.
    *   **Roleplay Elements:** Exploration is not merely about finding resources; it can lead to uncovering pieces of the world's lore, discovering unique items with special properties, learning new abilities, or encountering non-player characters (NPCs) who might offer quests, trade opportunities, or present new threats. Dungeons represent high-risk, high-reward areas, often guarded by unique bosses and containing valuable loot.

## 3. Unique Selling Propositions (USPs)

*   **Deep Automation in a Survival Context:** Unlike many survival games that feature relatively simple crafting systems, this game will incorporate in-depth factory building, logistics, and optimization mechanics reminiscent of titles like Factorio. These automation systems are not just a side activity but are directly and critically tied to meeting survival needs and establishing robust defenses.
*   **Meaningful Survival:** Basic survival needs (hydration, nourishment, rest) are not just abstract meters to be refilled. They are integral gameplay challenges that require dedicated automation chains and infrastructure (e.g., constructing water purifiers, setting up automated farming and food processing).
*   **Dynamic Threat Escalation:** Zombie attacks are not static or predictable. The threat will dynamically evolve based on multiple factors, including player progression through the tech tree, the size and pollution output of their factory (a nod to Factorio's threat generation), and special scripted or emergent game events. This ensures that players remain constantly challenged and must adapt their strategies.
*   **Integrated RPG Elements:** Exploration, dungeon-delving, and the discovery of lore and special items are not tangential activities. They offer tangible rewards that directly feed back into the core automation gameplay (e.g., finding new blueprints for advanced machines, acquiring rare materials for high-tier constructions) and enhance survival capabilities (e.g., obtaining powerful gear, unlocking unique character abilities).

## 4. Target Audience

This game aims to appeal to a diverse group of players, including:

*   Fans of complex automation and logistics games such as Factorio, Satisfactory, and Dyson Sphere Program.
*   Players who enjoy the challenge and emergent storytelling of survival crafting games like 7 Days to Die, Project Zomboid, and The Forest.
*   Gamers seeking a demanding and rewarding experience that effectively combines strategic thinking, meticulous resource management, and engaging action elements.
*   Players who appreciate games with significant depth, long-term progression paths, and replayability.

## 5. Roleplay Elements - Elaboration

To further enrich the player experience, several roleplaying elements will be woven into the core gameplay:

*   **Exploration:** The game world will feature a variety of distinct biomes, each with its own unique resources, environmental challenges, and visual identity. Scattered throughout these biomes will be Points of Interest (POIs) such as abandoned research laboratories, derelict military bunkers, or ancient, mysterious ruins. Investigating these locations can yield valuable lore fragments that piece together the world's backstory, rare crafting components, or trigger small, localized events.
*   **Dungeons:** These are specific, high-challenge, and often instanced or distinct areas designed for advanced players. Examples could include a sprawling underground cave system teeming with mutated creatures, a multi-level abandoned skyscraper now infested with a powerful zombie variant, or a forgotten pre-outbreak facility. Dungeons would feature unique environmental puzzles, tougher enemy variants, mini-boss encounters, and a culminating final boss. Successfully clearing a dungeon could reward players with unique artifact items, new advanced crafting recipes, or permanent character upgrades or abilities.
*   **Special Items/Abilities:**
    *   *Artifacts:* These are rare and powerful items, typically found through dedicated exploration of dangerous locations or as rewards for completing dungeons. Artifacts provide significant passive bonuses, such as increased crafting speed for specific categories of items, a small health regenerative aura for the player and nearby structures, or resistance to particular types of damage.
    *   *Active Abilities:* These could be unlocked through the main research tech tree or found as 'tech schematics' or 'mutated genes' within dungeons or high-tier POIs. Active abilities would provide short-duration buffs, unique combat maneuvers, or utility skills. Examples include a short-range directional teleport (blink), an electromagnetic pulse (EMP) blast to temporarily stun robotic enemies (if such an enemy type is included), or a temporary energy shield that absorbs incoming damage.
*   **Narrative/Lore:** The story of the world and the events leading to the zombie outbreak will primarily be delivered through environmental storytelling and discoverable lore items. These could take the form of personal diary entries, official reports, audio logs, or fragmented data logs found in computers within ruins and POIs. Piecing together this information will provide context, motivation for player actions, and a deeper understanding of the game world.

# Whitepaper - Part 2: Core Gameplay Mechanics

## 6. Automation Mechanics
    *   **Resource Gathering:**
        *   Initial: Manual gathering of basic resources (e.g., surface stone, wood, small ore deposits).
        *   Automated: Introduction of mining drills, pump jacks (for fluids), and other extractors that require power.
        *   Resource Nodes: Finite or infinite (slower regeneration) nodes of varying richness. Some rare resources may only be found in specific biomes or dungeons.
    *   **Processing & Refining:**
        *   Raw materials often need processing (e.g., ore into plates in furnaces, wood into planks in sawmills).
        *   Multi-stage processing for advanced materials (e.g., iron ore -> iron plates -> steel -> advanced machine parts).
        *   Fluid management: Water, oil, potentially chemical crafting. Pipes and pumps for transport.
    *   **Crafting Tiers & Assembly:**
        *   Hand crafting for basic items and initial machinery.
        *   Assembler machines for automated production of intermediate and advanced items.
        *   Tiered assemblers: Faster, more efficient, or capable of more complex recipes.
        *   Recipe complexity: Simple recipes for early game, increasingly complex recipes requiring multiple components for late-game items.
    *   **Factory Building & Logistics:**
        *   Conveyor belts, inserters (robotic arms) for item transport between machines.
        *   Logistics networks (e.g., drones, trains) for longer-distance transport as the factory grows.
        *   Power generation: From basic biomass burners to steam engines, solar, and potentially more advanced/sci-fi options. Power network management will be crucial.
        *   Base layout and optimization will be key to efficiency and defense.

## 7. Survival Mechanics
    *   **Basic Needs Management:**
        *   **Water:** Collect from sources (lakes, rain collectors). Boil/purify to make safe. Automated pumps and purifiers. Dehydration effects: stamina loss, reduced vision, eventual health loss.
        *   **Food:** Hunting animals, foraging plants, establishing farms. Cooking to improve nutritional value and safety. Automated hydroponics/protein synthesizers for late game. Starvation effects: reduced max health, slower movement, eventual health loss.
        *   **Sleep/Energy:** Designated bed or sleep structure. Lack of sleep leads to exhaustion: reduced crafting speed, accuracy penalties, blurred vision.
        *   **Shelter:** Building structures provides protection from zombies and potential environmental effects (e.g., storms, temperature extremes if implemented). Base integrity will be important.
    *   **Zombie AI & Wave Mechanics:**
        *   **AI Behavior:** Standard shamblers, faster variants, special infected (e.g., "tanks" that soak damage, "spitters" with ranged attacks, "screamers" that attract more zombies or buff others). Some zombies might target specific structures (e.g., power generators, food storage).
        *   **Wave Spawning:** Periodic timed waves. Special events (e.g., completing a dungeon, activating a "beacon") can trigger immediate, more challenging waves.
        *   **Pathfinding:** Zombies will attempt to find the most direct path to the player or critical base structures, potentially attacking walls or defenses in their way. Sound and light could attract them.
        *   **"Pollution" Mechanic (Factorio-like):** Factory activity (e.g., mining, power generation) could generate "threat" or "noise" that increases the intensity and frequency of zombie attacks over time.
    *   **Difficulty Scaling Factors:**
        *   **Time:** Baseline increase in difficulty as game days pass.
        *   **Player Progression:** Number of technologies researched, total factory output, or player level (if implemented) could scale raid strength.
        *   **Game Events:** Story-driven events or player-triggered events (e.g., activating an ancient artifact) could temporarily or permanently increase difficulty.
        *   **Exploration:** Discovering certain areas might unlock new, tougher zombie types.
    *   **Combat System:**
        *   **Player Combat:** Melee weapons (e.g., axe, crowbar, crafted swords), ranged weapons (e.g., bows, pistols, rifles, energy weapons). Ammunition will require crafting. Basic dodge/block mechanics.
        *   **Automated Defenses:** Walls, gates, turrets (ballistic, laser, flame), traps (mines, electric floors). These will require power and ammunition/maintenance.
        *   **Damage Types & Resistances:** Potential for different damage types (e.g., physical, fire, energy) and zombie resistances/vulnerabilities.

## 8. Progression Systems
    *   **Technology Tree:**
        *   Visual tree with unlockable tiers. Research requires "science packs" or similar items produced by the factory.
        *   Branches for automation, survival, combat, exploration, and potentially character upgrades.
        *   Some advanced technologies might require rare materials found through exploration or dungeons.
    *   **Item & Equipment Progression:**
        *   Tiered tools, weapons, and armor. Higher tiers offer better stats, durability, or special properties.
        *   Crafting recipes for these items are unlocked via the tech tree or by finding blueprints in the world.
    *   **Abilities & Skills:**
        *   If a skill system is implemented: Players could earn XP for actions (crafting, combat, exploration) and spend points on passive bonuses or active abilities.
        *   Alternatively, special abilities are unlocked via rare tech items or as rewards from dungeons (as mentioned in Part 1).
    *   **Exploration & Dungeon Rewards:**
        *   New, potent crafting materials (e.g., "mutated hearts" for bio-enhancements, "ancient alloy scraps" for powerful gear).
        *   Blueprints for unique machines, advanced weaponry, or special defensive structures.
        *   Artifacts providing global or personal buffs.
        *   Lore items that contribute to world understanding and potentially unlock new research paths.

## 9. World Systems
    *   **World Generation:**
        *   Likely procedural generation for replayability, using seeds.
        *   Configurable parameters (e.g., world size, resource richness, biome distribution, enemy density).
    *   **Biomes:**
        *   Distinct environments (e.g., temperate forest, arid desert, frozen tundra, toxic wasteland, ruined city).
        *   Each biome with unique resources, flora, fauna (neutral/hostile), weather patterns, and zombie variants.
        *   Some biomes may require specific gear or preparation to survive in (e.g., cold protection for tundra).
    *   **Points of Interest (POIs):**
        *   Varied structures: Abandoned shacks, crashed vehicles, old military outposts, research facilities, survivor encampments (potentially hostile or offering trade).
        *   Dungeons (as previously detailed) are a special type of POI.
        *   POIs contain loot, lore, potential environmental hazards, or small combat encounters.
    *   **Resource Distribution:**
        *   Basic resources (wood, stone, basic ores) available in starting areas.
        *   More advanced/rarer resources concentrated in specific biomes or deeper underground.
        *   Strategic resource placement to encourage exploration and factory expansion.

# Whitepaper - Part 3: Technical Design (Initial Thoughts)

## 10. Game Engine Proposal
    *   **Recommended Engine:** Unity or Unreal Engine.
        *   **Unity:**
            *   *Pros:* Strong 2D and 3D capabilities, large asset store, extensive documentation and community support, easier learning curve for C#, good for complex simulation and management games. Suitable for both top-down or isometric views often used in factory games, as well as first-person/third-person for survival aspects.
            *   *Cons:* Performance concerns for very large-scale simulations need careful management.
        *   **Unreal Engine:**
            *   *Pros:* High-end graphics capabilities, robust C++ framework, strong for 3D environments and complex AI. Blueprint system can speed up prototyping.
            *   *Cons:* Steeper learning curve, C++ can be more complex for some developers, potentially overkill if a simpler graphical style is chosen.
        *   **Godot Engine (Alternative):**
            *   *Pros:* Open-source and free, no licensing fees. Growing community. Good for 2D and increasingly capable for 3D. Uses GDScript (Python-like), C#, or C++.
            *   *Cons:* Smaller asset ecosystem compared to Unity/Unreal. Might require more custom tooling for very specific, advanced features.
    *   **Justification Criteria for Final Choice:**
        *   **Target Visuals:** (e.g., stylized 2D, isometric 3D, realistic 3D).
        *   **Team Expertise:** (Current or acquirable skills in C#, C++, or GDScript).
        *   **Performance Needs:** (Handling potentially thousands of entities in a large factory with complex logic and AI).
        *   **Multiplayer Ambitions:** (Native support or ease of integration for netcode).
        *   **Development Speed:** (Prototyping tools, asset availability).
    *   *(Initial Leaning: Unity might offer a good balance of flexibility for both Factorio-style top-down/isometric views and potentially more immersive first/third-person survival elements, along with a vast amount of readily available assets and tutorials.)*

## 11. High-Level System Architecture
    *   **Game Client (Single-Player Focus Initially):**
        *   **Game Engine Core:** Handles rendering, physics, input, audio.
        *   **World Manager:** Procedural generation, loading/saving world state, managing biomes and POIs.
        *   **Factory Manager:** Tracks all factory entities (machines, belts, inserters), manages their logic, updates production chains, power distribution.
        *   **Survival Manager:** Tracks player needs (health, hunger, thirst, sleep), environmental effects.
        *   **AI Manager:**
            *   *Zombie AI:* Pathfinding, behavior trees (idle, wander, seek, attack), wave management.
            *   *Fauna AI:* (If applicable) Neutral/hostile animal behaviors.
        *   **Player Controller:** Handles player input, movement, interaction with the world and UI.
        *   **Inventory & Crafting System:** Manages player and machine inventories, recipe execution.
        *   **UI/UX System:** Manages all user interface elements (HUD, menus, crafting screens, map).
        *   **Event System:** Global system for decoupled communication between different managers (e.g., "zombie wave starting" event, "research complete" event).
    *   **Multiplayer (Future Consideration):**
        *   If multiplayer is planned, a client-server architecture would be necessary.
        *   Authoritative server managing game state, client-side prediction for responsiveness.
        *   Dedicated server software or P2P with host migration. This significantly increases complexity.
    *   **Modularity:** Design systems to be as modular as possible to allow for easier debugging, updates, and potential future expansion (or modding support).

## 12. Data Management
    *   **Game State Serialization:**
        *   Saving and loading player progress, factory layout, world state (discovered areas, depleted resources).
        *   Format: JSON or binary format for efficiency.
        *   Need to handle versioning if game updates change save structures.
    *   **Game Configuration Data:**
        *   Item definitions, recipes, machine stats, zombie stats, biome parameters, loot tables.
        *   Likely stored in easily editable formats like JSON, XML, or ScriptableObjects (if Unity).
    *   **Player Profiles:** Settings, keybindings, achievements (if any).

## 13. Potential Technical Challenges and Risks
    *   **Performance at Scale:** Managing a large factory with thousands of moving parts, complex logic, and item tracking without significant performance degradation. This will require optimization techniques like object pooling, LOD (Level of Detail) for visuals and logic, and efficient data structures.
    *   **Complex AI:** Developing zombie AI that is challenging but fair, with good pathfinding in dynamically changing environments (player-built bases). Horde behavior and special zombie abilities add complexity.
    *   **Procedural World Generation:** Creating varied, interesting, and balanced worlds. Ensuring resource distribution supports gameplay progression.
    *   **Save/Load System Robustness:** Ensuring that complex game states can be saved and loaded reliably without corruption or exploits.
    *   **Balancing:** Fine-tuning the interplay between automation progression, survival needs, and zombie difficulty will be an ongoing challenge.
    *   **UI/UX for Complexity:** Designing an intuitive UI/UX that can handle the depth of Factorio-like automation and the demands of survival gameplay without overwhelming the player.
    *   **Scope Creep:** The combination of two deep genres (automation and survival) plus RPG elements creates a high risk of scope creep. Strict prioritization will be needed.
    *   **(If Multiplayer) Networking:** Implementing reliable and cheat-resistant multiplayer is a significant undertaking.
